Exercise sheets

Exercise Sheet 01 · Claims and their sources · Patterns as a language

This sheet brings together the two topics that open the term: *Claims and their sources* and *Patterns as a language*. You apply both to one and the same app from this week's top charts – first to the market claims that surround it, then to the patterns inside it. Every claim is hunted back to its oldest reachable source, and every pattern verdict names its price.

Sheet as PDF Slides for this session

Exercise Sheet 02 · Severity: formula and rubric · The fidelity decision

This sheet closes the analysis arc and opens your own project. In the first half you rank what actually hurts in the app you dissected on sheet 01, using the severity rubric the group built. In the second half you write your project's design question, put it under fire from your team, and decide which rung of the fidelity ladder answers it most cheaply. You leave the session with your fidelity block, the first half of what Part B presents.

Sheet as PDF Slides for this session

Exercise Sheet 03 · Building with the machine · Anchor I: The audit lab

This sheet is about what happens after the machine hands you a first screen. You drive your generated output into three recognizable states and keep a protocol of every decision while you work. Then the iterated UI goes into a peer audit along the audit grid, and you answer every finding in writing – accept with the change you will make, or refute with the argument. Unanswered findings are the only wrong option.

Sheet as PDF Slides for this session

Exercise Sheet 04 · Friction by design · Notifications: designing against the filter

This sheet puts both directions of interruption to your own prototype: the resistance it places in the user's way, and the interruptions it pushes into their day. You audit a team member's flow for friction and sort each point by whose interest it serves, then build a notification concept for your own project. Every friction point that only serves the organization has to be defended or dropped.

Sheet as PDF Slides for this session

Exercise Sheet 05 · Offline states and sync conflicts · Dark patterns: naming and judging

This sheet asks two questions of your own prototype: does it stay usable when the network disappears, and does it stay honest when the metrics tempt you? You triage which functions must survive offline, design the conflict case for your most critical data type, and file your own screens on the scale from legitimate to forbidden. Your partner's job is to attack both lists.
