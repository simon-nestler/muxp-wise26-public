3 principle

The claim that AI agents will replace apps is unsupported – and could still be true.

Both halves of this conflict hold, which makes it the semester's hardest. The agent thesis says that assistants will execute tasks, that the interface will migrate from screen to dialogue, and that the app as intermediary will disappear. Capital, keynotes and serious proponents stand behind it. As of August 2026, not one reliable usage-data point does. Measured reality shows record app usage (5.3 trillion hours in 2025) and AI apps arriving as a new category on top of that usage. Local-first had no usage data in 2019 either, and by 2026 it runs in production sync engines.

12 principle

Capital and keynotes back the agent thesis, and as of August 2026 no usage data does.

Capital and keynotes show that somebody is betting on the thesis. Usage data would show that people behave accordingly, and as of August 2026 there is none for substitution. Local-first sat in the same position in 2019.

14

The other direction: forecasts from this course's own slides

Local-first came true without data. The forecasts this course taught from until 2025 named no year, so none of them can lose.

  • Berkowski (2014), the book behind this course's market chapter: "Someone might launch a billion-dollar calendar app in the near future"
  • The same book asked whether the next generation of mobile browsers could challenge the app architecture, and gave no year for an answer
  • PayPal Beacon was announced on 09.09.2013 to "reinvent the in-store shopping experience"; this course's Forms slides still showed it in 2025
  • The contrast: the super-app thesis did get a dated verdict when Forrester declared its window closed on 14.07.2023
  • The agent thesis carries no date either. Ask of any thesis: what would falsify this, and by when?

Until the winter term 2025/26 this course opened with George Berkowski's How to Build a Billion Dollar App (2014). Its category chapter walks through what people do on their phones and says where the next billion-dollar app might come from. Calendars are "crying out for disruption", alarm apps might see "a big winner in this space in the near future", and the browser question stays a question. Twelve years on, nobody can say whether these forecasts failed, because "the near future" has no end date. The same chapter also carried dated figures, such as Skype with 300 million active users. A number like that can be checked, and it aged: Microsoft retired Skype on 5 May 2025.

The beacon wave of 2013 and 2014 had the same shape. Shops were to greet you by name and let you pay without taking out your phone, and the Forms slides of this course kept the Macy's and PayPal screens in their backup part until 2025. Whether the idea failed or moved quietly into other products is hard to say, because nobody had named a date. The Forrester verdict on super apps is the rare counter-case with a date attached.

The first two numbers come from Sensor Tower's State of Mobile 2026, the third from Forbes's May 2026 report on Sensor Tower and Similarweb data. 17 exercise

Opposing camps – research the side you like less Activity 1 · 25 minutes total

Task: One half of the room collects the strongest evidence for the agent thesis, the other half against – each person on the side they find less congenial (assigned by your hand at the start, or by the prediction you put on the Padlet). 15 min live research with source duty, then each camp presents its three best pieces – no claim theatre.

Time: 25 min total · Form: two camps, live

Result: 3 sourced pieces per camp

AI role: tool – declared, source-checked. Every number carries source and year, or it does not count.

The fairness rule of this activity is the semester's strictest: you collect for the side you find less congenial. Your hand at the start of the session shows which way you lean, and the assignment inverts it; whoever put a prediction on the Padlet after Topic 12 has already said it in writing. The rule has a practical point beyond fairness. Searching for a position you reject sends you to sources your own searches would never have opened, and it makes the weak spots of your own side visible from the outside.

The camps present

Three pieces per camp, source spoken aloud – the other camp audits the sources live.

  • The audit rule from the tribunal is routine now: location, source, year, or it does not count
  • The for camp, reliably, returns announcements, investments, capability demos
  • Usage, retention, revenue come back from the against camp

Reliably, the for camp comes back with announcements, investments and capability demos, and the against camp with usage, retention and revenue. The two camps seldom dispute each other's facts. They disagree about which kind of evidence says anything about the future, and the evidence-kinds map sorts exactly that disagreement.

Who profits from which prediction? Whoever sells agents needs substitution; whoever sells app-market data needs continuity.

Week 1's closing question returns one last time. The agent vendors need the substitution thesis, and the firms selling app-market data, Sensor Tower among them, need continuity. 22

The three prediction checks

Three checks for every prediction about mobile UX, before you believe it or wave it away.

  • 1 · Who says it, and what do they sell?
  • 2 · What would falsify it, and by when? (The 2014 forecasts in this course's slides never named one.)
  • 3 · What happens to my design if the claim is true, and if it is false?

The first check comes straight from the evidence-kinds map: before weighing a claim, find out what its source sells. The second is what Berkowski's "near future" lacked: the billion-dollar calendar app has neither arrived nor failed, because nobody had said by when it would have to come true. The third turns the question around and aims it at your own work. A design that only works if the agent thesis wins, or only if it loses, is a bet. A resilient design survives both answers, and the Epic/Apple installation flow in the appendix shows one, built to survive either regulatory outcome.

In the room the first two checks get a trial run right away. In pairs, take one of the three pieces the other camp presented and ask who says it and what would falsify it, by when. Two minutes, without devices.

Beacons, 2013/14: shopBeacon and PayPal Beacon

The last example in this course's old Forms slides was headed "Build truly mobile experiences ('forget the web')": Walmart's Scan & Go, Apple's EasyPay self-checkout, a QR-code toy store, and beacons. A beacon is a small Bluetooth Low Energy transmitter in a shop; the phone notices it and the app greets the customer, shows an offer or opens a payment. PayPal announced its Beacon on 9 September 2013 for hands-free in-store payment. The screen shows a shopBeacon welcome message in a Macy's store. The slides carried it into the winter term 2025/26 without a date, and the claim that shopping apps would work this way never received one either.

Sparring partner block the check question

Check question: Ask the model for current data on whether AI assistants are reducing app usage. Sort every offered item into the evidence-kinds map before accepting it: announcements will arrive dressed as data. If the model produces a usage-data point for substitution, open the source. As of August 2026 we could not find one, and checking whether that has changed is the exercise, freshly relevant every month.

Warning: This topic is where all the course's model-failure modes convene: recycled 2019 statistics with fresh dates, announcements cited as measurements, invented report titles, and a systematic lean toward the substitution narrative, told confidently in both directions. The vaccine is the full T01 discipline plus the evidence-kinds map, and the honest label where the record is empty.
